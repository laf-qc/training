# General course curriculum

Version: 0.1  
Planned learning time: **approximately 205 minutes**

Final assessment: **25 minutes**

Total: **approximately 3–4 hours**, depending on reading pace and practice

This is the common foundation. Examples deliberately rotate through the cheese room, cafe/restaurant, and shop so every learner recognises the risks before taking a department pathway.

## Learning outcomes by module

### 1. Food safety at La Fromagerie — 8 minutes

- Recognise that safe food is everyone's responsibility.
- Identify customers at greater risk without assuming only those customers can be harmed.
- Understand that training must be relevant to the work actually performed.
- Use the core rule: if the safe action is uncertain, pause the task and ask a manager.

### 2. Hazards and how food becomes unsafe — 12 minutes

- Classify hazards as microbiological, allergenic, chemical, or physical.
- Explain contamination, survival, and growth in simple terms.
- Recognise ready-to-eat food as especially dependent on preventing contamination.
- Spot department examples such as contaminated wrapping/knives, foreign bodies, cleaning spray, and undeclared allergens.

### 3. Personal hygiene and fitness for work — 12 minutes

- Demonstrate the approved handwashing sequence and know when it is required.
- Follow local rules for clothing, hair, jewellery, gloves, cuts, phones, eating, and smoking.
- Report vomiting, diarrhoea, infected wounds, and other relevant symptoms before handling food.
- Understand that gloves never replace clean hands or task changes.

### 4. Cross-contamination and allergen cross-contact — 16 minutes

- Trace contamination through hands, utensils, cloths, packaging, surfaces, storage, and food.
- Keep raw foods and ready-to-eat foods separate using La Fromagerie procedures.
- Distinguish microbial cross-contamination from allergen cross-contact while controlling both.
- Never guess allergen information; use the approved source and escalation route.
- Explain why removing a visible allergen does not make a dish safe.

### 5. Cleaning, disinfection, chemicals, and waste — 12 minutes

- Explain the difference between cleaning and disinfection.
- Follow the approved sequence, dilution, contact time, and cleaning schedule.
- Protect food and packaging from chemicals and dirty equipment.
- Manage cloths, waste, breakages, and pest evidence safely.

### 6. Time, temperature, chilling, cooking, and reheating — 16 minutes

- Explain why time and temperature control matter.
- Receive, store, prepare, cook, reheat, hot-hold, cool, and display food within approved procedures.
- Use, clean, disinfect, and check a food probe correctly where the role requires it.
- Record a check and take the defined corrective action when a limit is missed.
- Insert company-specific numeric limits only after procedure review; distinguish legal limits from company targets.

### 7. Safe delivery, storage, display, and stock rotation — 12 minutes

- Check condition, packaging, dates, contamination, pests, and temperature at delivery.
- Store food covered, labelled, separated, and in the correct place.
- Apply FIFO and distinguish use-by safety dates from best-before quality dates.
- Prevent customer, utensil, packaging, and display contamination.
- Treat damaged, returned, or suspect food according to the local hold/disposal procedure.

### 8. Safe systems, reporting, and speaking up — 12 minutes

- Describe the purpose of a food-safety management system based on HACCP principles.
- Complete required checks honestly and at the correct time.
- Know that a missed check must not be invented or backfilled.
- Isolate suspect food, preserve information, inform the responsible person, and record action.
- Respond appropriately to allergen concerns, contamination, refrigeration failure, pests, illness, and customer complaints.

## Recap model

Every module ends with:

- **Notice** — the hazard or warning sign.
- **Control** — the safe action expected.
- **Check** — how the learner knows the control worked.
- **Escalate** — when and to whom they report.

The final course recap groups the behaviours under the familiar food-hygiene themes of cleaning, cooking, chilling, and avoiding cross-contamination, with allergens, personal hygiene, storage, and safe-system reporting clearly visible rather than buried.

## Proposed visual set

1. **The contamination journey** — a raw-food/dirty-hand or packaging route moving through hand, knife, board, cloth, handle, and ready-to-eat product; learners identify every break point.
2. **Clean hands, safe task** — a six-stage handwashing sequence plus “wash now” triggers.
3. **Clean then disinfect** — visible dirt removal, rinse where required, disinfect at correct dilution/contact time, final rinse where required, air dry/ready for use.
4. **Cold storage has a logic** — covered/labelled foods, separation, airflow, monitoring point, and an alert/escalate state rather than invented universal shelf positions.
5. **The allergen conversation** — listen, check the approved information, communicate with the preparation team, prevent cross-contact, confirm, and never guess.

Final illustrations must reflect actual La Fromagerie equipment and procedures and must be reviewed before release.

## Required inputs before card copy is final

- Store names and which store has the limited hot-food offer.
- Current food-safety management system/SFBB pack and relevant safe methods.
- Company target and corrective-action temperatures.
- Sickness reporting and return-to-work rule.
- Handwashing, glove, jewellery, uniform, phone, and tasting policies.
- Allergen information source and verbal/written escalation script.
- Cleaning products, dilution/contact times, schedules, and colour coding (if any).
- Delivery rejection, date coding, opened-product shelf-life, quarantine, waste, and pest processes.
- Names/roles shown as escalation contacts and certificate authoriser.
