WHY FOOD SAFETY MATTERS

Every plate, wedge
& parcel is a promise.

Customers trust that the food we sell and serve will not harm them. That promise applies whether we cut a piece of cheese, prepare a sandwich, serve a restaurant dish or sell a sealed product.

Illness

Unsafe food can cause food poisoning or an allergic reaction.

Injury

Physical objects or chemicals can cause immediate harm.

Trust

One failure can affect a customer, the team and La Fromagerie’s reputation.

!

PEOPLE

WHO CAN BE HARMED?

Anyone can become ill.

Some people are more likely to become seriously ill from contaminated food: babies and young children, older people, pregnant people, and anyone whose immune system is weakened.

Do not try to judge risk by looking at a customer.Make every item safe for everyone.

SHARED RESPONSIBILITY

The business provides the system.
You follow it—and speak up.

LA FROMAGERIE

Food business responsibilities

  • Provide safe premises, equipment and working methods.
  • Identify hazards and maintain a food safety management system.
  • Give suitable instruction, training and supervision.
  • Provide accurate allergen information and safe food.
EVERY FOOD HANDLER

Your responsibilities

  • Follow the safe methods and training for your work.
  • Protect food from contamination.
  • Maintain personal hygiene and report relevant illness.
  • Report hazards, mistakes and failed controls immediately.
§

THE LAW

THE PRACTICAL MEANING

Food must be safe.

Food businesses must comply with food-safety and hygiene law, use food-safety procedures based on HACCP principles, and ensure food handlers are supervised, instructed and/or trained for the work they do.

For you: follow the procedure, complete required checks truthfully, and report anything unsafe.

For managers: put effective controls in place, train and supervise the team, act on problems, and keep appropriate records.

Legal framework overview: retained Regulation (EC) 852/2004 and the Food Safety and Hygiene (England) Regulations 2013, alongside food information/allergen requirements and later amendments.

✓

ENFORCEMENT

AUTHORISED OFFICERS

They verify that controls work.

Local-authority food-safety officers can inspect premises, examine food and records, investigate complaints or outbreaks, take samples, and require problems to be corrected.

Be open and truthful.Never hide, invent or backfill a check. Tell the responsible manager if a record or control has been missed.

WHEN CONTROLS FAIL

The consequences travel.

01A person

Illness, injury, distress or—in severe cases—death.

→
02The team

Investigation, lost time and damaged confidence.

→
03The business

Waste, closure, legal action, fines and loss of trust.

Reporting early gives the team the best chance to control the risk before food reaches a customer.

SCENARIOCHEESE ROOM

WHAT WOULD YOU DO?

The display fridge feels warmer than usual.

You are busy, there is a queue, and no manager is standing beside you. The cheeses look and smell normal.

Safe decision

Pause. Protect the food, follow the temperature-check procedure, and report the concern to the responsible person. Appearance and smell cannot confirm that food is safe.

A HAZARD CAN CAUSE HARM

Four types.
One safe response.

MMicrobiological

Harmful bacteria, viruses, moulds or parasites.

AAllergenic

An allergen present, transferred or not declared.

CChemical

Cleaning products, pest chemicals or unsuitable materials.

PPhysical

Glass, metal, stones, plastic, wood or other objects.

Notice it → protect the food → report it → follow the safe method.

MICROBIOLOGICAL HAZARDS

Too small
to see.

Pathogens are microorganisms that can cause disease. Food may look, smell and taste normal while carrying enough harmful microorganisms—or their toxins—to make someone ill.

  • BacteriaExamples include Salmonella, Campylobacter, harmful E. coli and Listeria.
  • VirusesNorovirus can reach food from an infected person or contaminated surfaces.
  • Toxins and sporesSome microorganisms form resistant spores or produce toxins; ordinary appearance is not a safety test.
!

FOOD POISONING

ILLNESS CAUSED BY CONTAMINATED FOOD

It is more than
a “bad stomach”.

Common symptoms include nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, stomach cramps and fever. Symptoms may begin quickly or take days, and serious cases can require hospital care or be fatal.

You cannot prove food is safe by smelling or tasting it.Correct handling, time and temperature controls, separation, cleaning and records provide the evidence.

HOW BACTERIA MULTIPLY

Give them the conditions,
and numbers can rise quickly.

01Food

Many bacteria grow well in moist, protein-rich foods.

02Moisture

Water supports growth; dry conditions often slow it.

03Warmth

Growth is faster in favourable temperatures.

04Time

The longer conditions remain favourable, the greater the opportunity to multiply.

Important: chilling slows the growth of many bacteria; it does not reliably kill them. Temperature limits and checks are covered in Module 6.

RTE

READY TO EAT

FOODS NEED DIFFERENT CONTROLS

No further cooking
means no final kill step.

Ready-to-eat food will be eaten without further cooking that would remove harmful microorganisms. It must be protected from contamination throughout storage, preparation, display and service.

Cut cheeseSandwichesCharcuterieSaladsCooked quicheDesserts

Some chilled ready-to-eat foods—including soft mould-ripened cheese and deli meats—need especially careful control because they can be associated with Listeria.

C

CHEMICAL

SUBSTANCES WHERE THEY SHOULD NOT BE

Cleaners clean.
They do not belong in food.

  • ExamplesCleaning chemicals, sanitiser at the wrong dilution, pest-control products, machine lubricants, or chemicals stored in food containers.
  • ControlsUse labelled products as instructed, keep them away from food, observe dilution/contact instructions, and never decant them into food or drink containers.
  • If suspectedStop, isolate the food and tell the responsible person. Do not taste it.
P

PHYSICAL

OBJECTS THAT CAN INJURE OR CHOKE

Foreign objects
have many routes.

Broken glassMetal fragmentsHard plasticWood splintersStones or gritPackaging piecesJewelleryHair

Prevent damage, maintain equipment, inspect food and packaging, control personal items, and follow the breakage procedure. Never pick out one visible piece and assume the rest is safe.

A

ALLERGENIC

INGREDIENT · TRANSFER · INFORMATION

For some customers,
a trace can matter.

An allergenic hazard exists when an allergen is present unexpectedly, transfers between foods, or is not communicated accurately. Milk is central to much of our work, but it is only one of the 14 allergens that food businesses must declare.

Never guess or promise “allergen-free”.Check the approved information and follow La Fromagerie’s allergen procedure. If you cannot confirm, stop and ask.

A hazard can travel
without moving itself.

1Source

→
2Vehicle

Hands, knife, cloth, board, handle, packaging or surface

→
3Food

A ready-to-eat item that receives no further control

Break the route with separation, handwashing, cleaning and disinfection, protected storage, correct equipment and accurate information.

NAME THE HAZARD BEFORE CHOOSING THE CONTROL

  • CafeSanitiser is sprayed beside uncovered prepared food. Chemical.

PEOPLE & FOOD

Hands are useful.
They are also a route.

Microorganisms and other hazards can pass from our bodies, clothing and personal belongings to food—directly or through equipment and high-touch surfaces.

  • HandsCan transfer contamination after the toilet, raw food, waste, cleaning or touching the face.
  • Clothing & hairCan carry dirt, hairs and microorganisms into food areas.
  • IllnessAn infected food handler can contaminate food even when trying to work carefully.

HANDWASHING · WHEN

Wash before clean work.
Wash after contamination.

Before

Starting food preparation, handling ready-to-eat food, putting on clean gloves, or returning to a clean task.

After

Using the toilet; raw food; bins or waste; cleaning; coughing, sneezing or blowing your nose; touching your face or hair.

Also after

Phones, door handles, light switches, cash registers, money, deliveries, breaks—or whenever hands may be contaminated.

Use the designated handwash basin with warm running water, soap and hygienic drying. Never use a food-preparation sink.

HANDWASHING · METHOD

Every surface.
Every time.

  1. 01Wet

    Wet hands under warm running water.

  2. 02Soap

    Apply enough liquid soap to cover both hands.

  3. 03Rub

    Palms, backs, between fingers, fingertips, thumbs and wrists.

  4. 04Rinse

    Rinse away soap and loosened contamination.

  5. 05Dry

    Dry thoroughly with a disposable towel.

If needed, use the towel to turn off a hand-operated tap and avoid recontaminating clean hands.

✓

ARRIVE READY

CLOTHING · HAIR · PERSONAL ITEMS

Clean, covered
& controlled.

  • ClothingWear clean, suitable protective clothing as required for your role. Change it if contaminated.
  • HairTie hair back and use the required head covering. Keep beards controlled where your procedure requires it.
  • JewelleryDo not wear watches or jewellery in food-handling areas, except a plain wedding band where permitted.
  • BelongingsKeep phones, bags and outdoor clothing away from food and food-contact equipment.
×

FALSE SECURITY

SINGLE-USE GLOVES

Gloves do not replace
handwashing.

Gloves can become contaminated just like hands. Wash and dry hands before putting them on, use them only where the safe method requires them, and change them between tasks or whenever torn or contaminated.

Never wash or reuse single-use gloves.Remove them safely, dispose of them, wash your hands, then use a fresh pair if the task requires it.
+

CUTS & SORES

REPORT · COVER · PROTECT

A small cut still
needs a safe response.

Tell the responsible person. Completely cover cuts and sores with a brightly coloured, waterproof dressing. If the wound cannot be protected, you must not handle open food.

Lost dressing?Stop immediately, protect affected food and report it. Follow the foreign-body procedure—do not simply replace it and continue.

SAFE BEHAVIOUR

Keep habits away
from food.

No eating or chewing gumNo smoking or vapingNo spittingDo not touch face or hairCough or sneeze away from foodWash hands after tissues

If you cough or sneeze near exposed food, stop. Protect or dispose of food as directed, clean affected surfaces and wash your hands before returning.

!

FITNESS FOR WORK

REPORT BEFORE HANDLING FOOD

Do not “push through”
vomiting or diarrhoea.

Tell your manager immediately if you have vomiting, diarrhoea, or another illness or infection that could contaminate food. Report symptoms before arriving where possible—and report them if they begin during a shift.

Early reporting protects everyone.Your manager will decide whether you must go home, avoid open food, or carry out different duties.

EXCLUSION & RETURN TO WORK

48 hours symptom-free
is the usual minimum.

1Report

Tell the manager about vomiting or diarrhoea immediately.

2Stay away

Do not handle or work around open food while excluded.

3Count correctly

Wait at least 48 hours after symptoms stop naturally—not after medicine masks them.

4Confirm

Speak to the manager before returning. Some diagnoses require different or longer action.

SCENARIOSTART OF SHIFT

WHAT WOULD YOU DO?

but feel better now.

The restaurant is short-staffed and you believe you could work carefully with gloves.

Safe decision

Do not start food work. Report the illness immediately and follow the manager’s instruction. Gloves cannot make an ill food handler safe, and the usual exclusion is at least 48 hours after symptoms stop naturally.

PERSONAL HYGIENE TRAVELS WITH YOU

the next task.

  • Cheese roomAfter taking payment, wash hands before cutting or wrapping open cheese.
  • CafeAfter handling raw ingredients, wash hands and change contaminated protective clothing before ready-to-eat work.
  • ShopAfter bins, deliveries or cleaning, wash hands before handling sandwiches, quiche or service equipment.

Changing task means reassessing your hands, clothing, tools and work area.

CROSS-CONTAMINATION

A hazard moves
from one place to another.

1Source

Raw food, allergen, person, waste, packaging or contaminated surface

→
2Route

Hands, cloths, knives, boards, slicers, containers, tongs or drips

→
3Recipient

Ready-to-eat food, clean equipment or another ingredient

Direct contact needs no middle step. Indirect contact uses a person, surface, tool or droplet as the vehicle.

MICROBIOLOGICAL SEPARATION

Raw stays away
from ready to eat.

RAW / CONTAMINATEDKeep contained

Store covered and below ready-to-eat food. Control packaging, drips and the route through the workplace.

PEOPLE / EQUIPMENTReset the route

Wash hands and use the correct cleaned and disinfected equipment before changing to ready-to-eat work.

READY TO EATProtect until service

Cover, label and use dedicated areas or equipment where the safe method requires them.

!

ALLERGEN RISK

ALLERGY · INTOLERANCE · COELIAC DISEASE

A small amount
can cause serious harm.

A food allergy involves the immune system and can cause a severe, potentially fatal reaction. Food intolerance is different, while coeliac disease is an autoimmune condition triggered by gluten. Every request must be taken seriously.

Never decide what is “safe enough” for the customer.Use current approved information, follow the allergen procedure, and be honest about cross-contact risk.

THE 14 REGULATED ALLERGENS

Know the names.
Check the ingredients.

CeleryCereals containing glutenCrustaceansEggsFishLupinMilkMolluscsMustardPeanutsSesameSoybeansSulphur dioxide & sulphites*Tree nuts

*When sulphur dioxide and sulphites are present above the legal threshold. People may also react to foods outside these 14.

INFORMATION CONTROL

Recipe memory
is not evidence.

Allergen information must follow the actual food being served or sold. Products, suppliers, garnishes, sauces, stocks, oils and recipes can change.

  • CheckThe current label, recipe, specification or approved allergen record.
  • UpdateInformation whenever an ingredient, supplier, recipe or preparation method changes.
  • StopIf information is missing, unclear, damaged or contradictory. Do not substitute from memory.

WHEN A CUSTOMER TELLS YOU ABOUT AN ALLERGY

Listen. Check.
Communicate.

  1. 01Listen carefully

    Confirm which food or ingredient the customer needs to avoid.

  2. 02Tell the right person

    Follow the store procedure and communicate clearly to the kitchen or responsible colleague.

  3. 03Check approved information

    Include every ingredient and relevant cross-contact risk. Never rely on memory.

  4. 04Answer honestly

    If safety cannot be confirmed, say so. Never promise “allergen-free” without an authorised basis.

HOW FOOD IS OFFERED CHANGES THE INFORMATION

Loose, prepacked
or PPDS?

Non-prepacked / loose

Allergen information for the 14 must be available. Written information supported by a conversation is FSA best practice.

Prepacked

Use the manufacturer’s intact label. Allergens are emphasised in the ingredients list. Never cover or discard required information.

Prepacked for direct sale

Food packed on the same premises before the customer selects or orders it needs the food name, full ingredients list and emphasised allergens.

ALLERGEN CROSS-CONTACT CONTROLS

Separate the food.
Reset the route.

01Store

Keep ingredients closed, labelled and separated to prevent spills and mix-ups.

02Prepare

Use the agreed area, sequence and dedicated or correctly cleaned equipment.

03Hands

Wash thoroughly between allergen-containing and requested food. Change gloves if used.

04Serve

Identify the correct order, protect it from contact and communicate it clearly.

VISIBLE IS NOT ENOUGH

REMOVE THE ALLERGEN

A quick wipe
is not a safe reset.

Crumbs, powder, oils and residues can remain on hands, cloths, boards, knives, slicers, tongs and containers. Follow the approved cleaning method before preparing food for an allergy request.

Cleaning and disinfection have different jobs.For allergen control, the residue must be physically removed. Do not spread it with a contaminated cloth.
HAZARD ROUTESTHREE DEPARTMENTS

BREAK THE ROUTE BEFORE SERVICE

Where could it travel?

  • Cheese roomA knife used on walnut-coated cheese is used on another cheese after only being wiped.
  • CafeThe same fryer oil is used for battered fish and an order described as gluten-free.
  • ShopSesame seeds spill above uncovered sandwiches and their labels.

Stop, isolate affected food, clean correctly, verify information and tell the responsible person.

!

BEFORE IT REACHES THE CUSTOMER

MISTAKE · UNCERTAINTY · WRONG ORDER

Do not remove the garnish
and send it back.

If an allergen, wrong ingredient or contaminated utensil has touched the food, stop service and tell the responsible person. The food must be remade safely using the agreed procedure—not picked off, wiped or transferred to a clean plate.

If food may already have reached the customer:Alert the manager immediately and follow the emergency procedure. Do not minimise or conceal what happened.
